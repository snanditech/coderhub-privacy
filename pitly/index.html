Pitly Privacy Policy

App: Pitly (pitly) · Developer: CoderHub26 · Effective: 14 November 2026

Summary

Pitly is an offline board for three sowing games: Kalah, Oware and a documented Pallanguzhi home variant, with bots and two-player pass-and-play. It has no account, no developer server and no INTERNET permission. There are no online or Wi-Fi rooms, chat or leaderboards.

What the app stores on your device

The app keeps the following in private storage under files/pitly:

  • match.json (with a local .bak copy): the current board, variant, difficulty, seed/rng state, pits, stores, turn, bots, move count, last move/capture flags, finished flag and winner.
  • stats.json: per-variant games played, wins, losses, draws and best store.
  • Private preferences: theme, default bot difficulty, fast-bots, large-board and hint switches.

The files are written atomically and keep a local backup. They are not separately encrypted; they rely on Android's app sandbox and device storage encryption.

What leaves the device

Nothing is uploaded. The app declares no INTERNET permission and contains no developer server, advertising, analytics or tracking code, so it cannot send your content to the developer or an ad network.

Pitly has no share sheet, no export and no network feature. Your saves and statistics are not sent anywhere.

Permissions

The app declares no Android runtime permission. It does not request storage, location, camera, microphone, contacts or notification access.

Like any Android app built with AndroidX, the merged manifest also contains the library's signature-level DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION. It is used internally for broadcast receivers, is granted only to this app's own signature, and gives no access to your data.

Android backup and device transfer

Android automatic backup is turned off for this app (the manifest sets allowBackup=false and fullBackupContent=false). Its current extraction rules exclude only the database and sharedpref domains from cloud backup; the app's files/ records are not named in a separate rule. The file also has no device-transfer block. The manifest switch is the primary control, and the extraction XML should be widened if backup is ever enabled.

Rules limits

Kalah and Oware follow the documented rules in Help. Pallanguzhi is an explicit home variant, not a claim to every regional Pallanguzhi ruleset. The app does not implement repetition detection; a 600-move safety stop ends a no-progress game as documented. A regional rules specialist should review the Oware feeding/grand-slam/start-skip rules and the Pallanguzhi variant before release.

Ads, accounts and purchases

The app has no user accounts and no sign-in. There are no ads, no in-app purchases and no advertising, analytics or billing SDK. The developer does not collect, receive or sell any data from the app.

Children

Pitly is not directed at children under 13. It has no accounts and no way to send information to the developer, so it does not knowingly collect personal information from children or anyone else.

Data deletion

Reset statistics in Settings and clear the saved match from the board or by starting a new game. Clearing the app's storage from Android Settings > Apps > Pitly > Storage deletes both files. Uninstalling the app deletes its private storage. There is no exported or server copy.

Changes

If this policy changes, the updated version is posted on this page with a new effective date. The app itself does not need to change for a policy update.
